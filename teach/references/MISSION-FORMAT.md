# MISSION.md Format

`MISSION.md` lives at the workspace root. It captures the _reason_ the learner is chasing this topic. Every teaching decision — what to teach next, which resources to surface, which drills to design — traces back to this document. Without it, lessons feel abstract and you have no way to judge what comes next.

## Template

```md
# Mission: {Topic}

## Why
{1-3 sentences. The concrete real-world goal. What changes in their life or work when
they have this skill? Avoid "to understand X" — push for the underlying outcome.}

## Success looks like
- {A specific, observable thing the learner will be able to do}
- {Another specific thing}

## Constraints
- {Time, budget, prior commitments, learning preferences — anything that bounds the approach}

## Out of scope
- {Adjacent topics they explicitly do not want right now — protects the zone of proximal development}
```

## Rules

- **One mission per workspace.** Two unrelated goals means two workspaces.
- **Concrete over abstract.** "Solve a scrambled 3x3 unaided by Friday" beats "get good at cubing." "Ship a Rust CLI to my team" beats "learn Rust."
- **Push back on vagueness.** If the learner cannot say _why_, interview them before writing anything. A bad mission is worse than none.
- **Revise when reality shifts.** Missions move as skill grows. Update the file (confirm first) and write a learning record capturing the shift. Don't leave a stale mission steering future sessions.
- **Keep it short.** Past one screen it has stopped being a compass and become a plan.
