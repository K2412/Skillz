# GLOSSARY.md Format

`GLOSSARY.md` is the canonical language of the workspace. Every lesson, drill, and learning record adheres to its terminology. Building it is itself learning: compressing a concept into a tight definition is _evidence_ the learner understands it. Once a glossary exists, later lessons get shorter — they can lean on defined terms instead of re-explaining.

## Structure

```md
# {Topic} Glossary

{One or two sentences on what this glossary covers.}

## Terms

**Progressive overload**:
Systematically increasing the demand on a muscle over time — via load, volume, or intensity.
_Avoid_: pushing harder, levelling up

**RPE (Rate of Perceived Exertion)**:
A 1–10 self-rating of how hard a set felt, where 10 is failure and 8 means two reps left.
_Avoid_: effort score, intensity rating
```

## Rules

- **Add a term only once the learner understands it.** The glossary records compressed knowledge — it is not a dictionary they read to learn. Wait until they can use the term correctly before promoting it.
- **Be opinionated.** When several words mean the same thing, pick the best and list the rest as aliases to avoid. That is how language compresses.
- **Keep definitions tight.** One or two sentences. Define what the term IS, not how to do it.
- **Use the glossary's own terms inside definitions.** This is what makes hard terms easier later.
- **Group under subheadings** when natural clusters emerge (`## Anatomy`, `## Notation`). A flat list is fine when terms cohere.
- **Flag ambiguities.** "In this workspace, 'set' always means a working set — warm-ups are tracked separately."
- **Revise as understanding deepens.** Update stale entries in place.
